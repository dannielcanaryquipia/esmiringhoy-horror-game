# Esmiringhoy — The Story

By Danniel Canary Quipia · October 2026

The full story bible for Esmiringhoy: the lore, characters, notes, prayer and endings that the game's text, tasks and trailer draw from.

## Premise

For 139 years the Villareal family has paid for its fortune by keeping a demon asleep under their house. The last keeper, Lola Remedios, has just died. On the third night of her wake, during a typhoon, the candles around her coffin go out on their own and the Esmiringhoy wakes.

The cousins who stayed the night are trapped inside with it. Lola left them one way out: light five blessed candles in the old prayer room, say the prayer her grandmother wrote, and end the bargain for good. They have until dawn. Anyone the Esmiringhoy catches becomes one of its children and hunts the others.

**Hook:** *Your lola kept it asleep for sixty years. Tonight it is your turn, and keeping it is not enough. You have to finish it.*

## Setting

**Casa Villareal** is a two-storey *bahay na bato* built in 1878 at the edge of **San Rafael de la Costa**, a fictional fishing and abaca town on the southern Luzon coast. The ground floor is cold stone: the *zaguan* (entrance hall) with an old carriage, the *bodega* (storage room) full of *tapayan* (big clay jars), and the *cuadra* (stable). The upper floor is narra wood and capiz windows: the *caida* (landing), the *sala* with a piano and grandfather clock, the *comedor* (dining room), the *kusina* (kitchen), the *baño* (bathroom), the children's room, Lola's bedroom (*Ang Silid*) and the locked *oratorio* (private prayer room).

The town believes the house is lucky. Every Villareal generation has prospered while the rest of San Rafael stayed poor. The older townspeople say otherwise, quietly. They cross themselves when they pass the gate, and nobody will work there after dark.

**Tonight:** the third night of Lola Remedios's *lamay* (wake). Her coffin lies in the sala under a ring of white candles. A late-season typhoon has cut the power and the phone signal. The mourners have gone home before the road floods, and six cousins have stayed behind to keep vigil. At 12:00 the clock stops. At 12:03 every candle in the house goes out at once.

The Esmiringhoy has been in the house since 1887, held asleep by one keeper per generation. Every one of these events leaves something the players can find.

Nobody alive remembers what *Esmiringhoy* means. It is the name the old servants whispered, and it was already old when they used it.

## The Esmiringhoy

It is not the ghost of a person. It is older than the house, something that lived under the stone long before the Villareals built on top of it. Don Teodoro gave it a door. Every keeper since has only kept that door shut.

It is 2.4 metres tall and stoops under every doorway. Its skin is charred and split like burnt narra, with ember light pulsing in the cracks: slowly when it is calm, fast when it hunts. Horns sweep back from a skull-like face with empty sockets. Two red points light up in them when it has seen you. Its arms reach its ankles, and its claws leave scorch marks that glow on the floorboards and fade over about twenty seconds.

**Its rules.** Each one is a mechanic, so the lore teaches the player how to survive.

| Rule | In the story | In the game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Still light holds it | Isabel wrote that "it cannot bear to be looked at" | Holding the flashlight on it freezes it for up to 2.5 s; moonlit patches block its path |
| The candle circle | Blessed candles burn with a light it cannot cross | A lit ritual candle makes a 2 m safe zone |
| Light gives you away | It "sees the lamps of the living through walls" (Yaya Ising) | It notices a lit flashlight from 20 m |
| It hears breath | Tomasito's drawing: "he listens for breathing" | Hold your breath when it is near your hiding spot |
| It remembers | "It never looks in the same place twice. It looks where you went twice." (Lola) | Hiding spots you reuse get checked first |
| It wears what it takes | Those it catches become its *mga anak* (children) | Caught players turn into lesser demons with orange eyes and hunt the others |
| The house warns you | "The house was ours before it was his. It still tries to warn us." (Lola) | The bulb dies, the chair rocks and the kulambo sways when it is near |
| Dawn decides | The bargain renews at sunrise | If dawn comes before the rite is finished, the survivors become the new keepers, forever |

**How it grows.** Each candle lit in the oratorio wounds it and makes it angrier. After the first candle it breaks doors. After the third it crawls along the ceilings. After the fourth it steps out of mirrors.

## Characters

**The six cousins (playable).** In solo play you are Ana. In co-op each player picks a cousin. The differences are story only: their lines, their reactions to notes, and what they say when they are caught.

| Cousin | Age | Who they are | Their line when caught |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ana Villareal** | 22 | The youngest grandchild and Lola's favourite. Lola left the letter to her. A nursing student in Manila who came home for the wake. | "Lola, sorry… hindi ko natapos." ("Lola, I'm sorry… I didn't finish it.") |
| **Isko Villareal** | 34 | The eldest grandson. An electrical engineer; practical and calm, and the one who knows where the fuse box is. Doesn't believe any of this until the lights die. | "Takbo! Huwag kayong lumingon!" ("Run! Don't look back!") |
| **Mara Ocampo** | 28 | A vlogger cousin from the city who wanted to film the lamay for her channel. Her camera's flash is how the group first sees it. | "Naka-record… lahat naka-record…" ("It's recording… it's all recording…") |
| **Bea Villareal** | 30 | A nurse home from abroad for the funeral. Steady under pressure; the one who keeps everyone breathing quietly. | "Huwag kayong huminga…" ("Don't breathe…") |
| **Tess Villareal** | 41 | The devout aunt who led the novena every night. She knows Lola's prayers by heart, and suspected all along. | "Diyos ko…" ("My God…") |
| **Paolo Villareal** | 37 | The black sheep. He came hoping to talk about the inheritance and is the first to try the locked front door. | "Akin na lang ang bahay… akin na…" ("The house can be mine… mine…") |

**The family of the past (found in notes).**

- **Don Teodoro Villareal (1841–1902):** made the bargain in 1887. His diary turns from desperate to proud to terrified.
- **Doña Isabel Villareal (1848–1889):** his wife and the first keeper. She wrote the Oración and died before she could finish the rite.
- **Padre Anselmo:** the parish priest who blessed the five candles in five churches and swore never to speak of the house again.
- **Tomasito Villareal (1916–1923):** the boy who vanished. His music box still plays the song his mother hummed. In the true ending he is among the children set free.
- **Yaya Ising (Luisa Dimaano):** the family cook in 1945. She left the house the night she saw it and wrote down everything.
- **Lola Remedios Villareal (1940–2026):** keeper for 61 years. Strict, loving, always tired, and never explained why. Her notes guide the player through the whole night.

## The night, act by act

A full run takes 20–35 minutes. Each act ends on a beat the player feels, not a cutscene.

1. **Prologue: Ang Lamay (11:40 PM, playable, about 2 minutes).** Rain drums on the capiz windows. The cousins sit around Lola's coffin in the sala, half asleep. Tess leads one last prayer. Paolo jokes about the inheritance. At 12:00 the grandfather clock stops mid-tick. At 12:03 every candle in the house goes out at once, and in the dark something below the floor exhales. Cut to black.
2. **Act I: Dilim (Darkness).** Ana wakes alone on the cold stone of the zaguan. The front door is barred from outside. On the carriage step: her flashlight and Lola's letter (Note 1). The others are scattered through the house; in co-op, each player wakes in a different room. Tasks: find matches, then go down into the bodega for three fuses. Turning the power back on is the first big mistake every player makes: the whole house hums, and upstairs something starts walking.
3. **Act II: Ang Limang Kandila (The Five Candles).** The cousins search the house for the five blessed candles Lola hid. Each one is behind a task tied to a family memory: the grandfather clock stopped at Lola's time of death, Tomasito's music box, the black water in the tub, the symbol in Lola's mirror, Doña Isabel's song on the piano, and the clay jars in the bodega. The notes explain why each hiding place matters. With every candle found, the Esmiringhoy gets closer and smarter. This is where hiding under beds, holding your breath and reading the house's warnings become survival.
4. **Act III: Ang Oración (The Prayer).** With the three torn pages, the cousins learn the order of the candles and open the oratorio, using a key split between the clay jars and the faceless santo's hands. Lighting the candles in the right order wounds the Esmiringhoy. It roars through the whole house and comes to the doorway. The final rite: 40 seconds of prayer while it blows the candles out from the threshold, one by one, and you relight them with shaking hands and your last matches.
5. **Epilogue: Bago Mag-umaga (Before Dawn).** The prayer ends and the barred front door bursts open. The house fights back as the cousins run: doors slam, the gallery bulbs explode and the grand stair collapses, forcing a desperate loop down the back stair. Outside, the typhoon has passed, and the first grey light is on the road.

**In co-op:** any caught cousin becomes one of its *anak* and hunts the others. That's tragic, but not final. If the survivors finish the rite, every turned cousin wakes up human on the zaguan floor at dawn, coughing ash.

## The notes

Eight notes, in the order players usually find them. The puzzle answers in **bold** are the defaults; the game randomizes them per run.

**1. Lola's letter to Ana** (zaguan, on the carriage step)

> Ana, apo. If you are reading this, I am gone and the candles are out. I am sorry. I kept it asleep for sixty-one years, and I was too afraid to finish what Doña Isabel started. Do not keep it. Finish it. Five candles, the prayer, the oratorio. Leave before the sun comes up. Do not run when you can hide. Do not hide where you have hidden before. Mahal kita. — Lola

**2. Lola's death certificate** (Ang Silid, vanity drawer)

> Remedios Villareal, 86. Cause of death: cardiac arrest. Time of death: **3:15 AM**. *In Lola's handwriting in the margin:* "The clock in the sala will stop at my hour. Wind it back to me."

**3. Don Teodoro's diary, 1887** (bodega, locked chest)

> 14 October. The storm took the last of the abaca. The bank will take the house. Tonight I went down to the silong with a lamp, and something under the old stones spoke my name. It asked for so little. One of my blood each generation, and someone to keep it sleeping. I said yes. God forgive me, I said yes before it finished asking.

**4. Padre Anselmo's letter, 1888** (sala, inside the piano bench)

> Doña Isabel, the five candles are blessed, one in each of the five churches, as you asked. Lit each night, they will keep it sleeping. But to send it back, they must be lit in the oratorio *in the order written on your pages*, and the Oración said to the end without stopping. Tell no one. I will not come to the house again.

**5. Tomasito's drawing, 1923** (children's room, under the small bed)

> A crayon drawing of a tall black man with orange cracks, bent under a door, with a boy hiding under a bed. In a child's letters: *"Ang matangkad na lalaki sa ilalim ng sahig. Nakikinig siya kung humihinga ka."* ("The tall man under the floor. He listens to see if you are breathing.") On the back: *"Tinago ni Yaya ang susi ng music box ko sa lagayan ng asukal."* ("Yaya hid the key of my music box in the sugar jar.")

**6. Yaya Ising's account, 1945** (kusina, behind a loose tile)

> I saw it in the kitchen doorway, taller than the door, bent like a broken umbrella. It did not come in. It was listening to the piano upstairs, Señora Isabel's old song that the Señorita plays: **mi, do, sol, la**. It loves that song. I think it remembers her. I am leaving tonight and I am never coming back.

**7. Lola's ledger of nights** (Ang Silid, under the altar cloth)

> 22,265 nights. Five candles each night. My knees are gone. Note for whoever comes after: the candle I keep on the altar is in the drawer the mirror shows you, but only in the dark. Turn your light off. The mirror remembers what the light forgets.

**8. Lola's last page** (sala, folded in her hands in the coffin, with her rosary)

> It is waking already; I can hear it in the floor. The key to the oratorio is in two halves: one in the jars below, one in the hands of the santo who has no face. Tell the others I was strict because I was afraid. Tell them I loved them. Then light the candles and do not stop praying until it is gone.

## The Oración

Doña Isabel's prayer, torn into three pages and hidden in different rooms (randomized per run). Each page carries a fragment of the prayer and one or two of the five symbols carved on the oratorio's candle holders. Together they give the lighting order. The prayer is original to this game, written in Tagalog with an English line beneath. During the final rite its lines appear on screen one by one as the player recites.

**Page I**: symbols: *the moon, the key*

> Sa liwanag ng limang kandila, — *By the light of five candles,*
>
> bumalik ka sa dilim na pinagmulan mo. — *go back to the darkness you came from.*

**Page II**: symbols: *the bell, the eye*

> Wala kang bahay dito. — *You have no house here.*
>
> Wala kang dugo dito. — *You have no blood here.*
>
> Ang utang ay bayad na. — *The debt is paid.*

**Page III**: symbol: *the open hand*

> Umalis ka, at huwag ka nang bumalik. — *Leave, and never come back.*
>
> At ang mga kinuha mo, ibalik mo sa amin. — *And those you took, give them back to us.*

The last line is why the true ending frees the turned cousins, and Tomasito.

The death screen reuses the turned theme with one line, ***Natagpuan ka.*** (*You were found.*), followed by the reason ("It saw you hide.", "It heard you breathe.", "It remembered your hiding place.").

## Tone, themes and blurb

**Tone.** Quiet dread, not gore. The scares come from sound, darkness and waiting: footsteps that stop, a light that dies, a face appearing sideways at the edge of the bed. The house should feel like a real Filipino home that has gone wrong: rosaries, plastic-covered sofa, the smell of candle wax and rain. Violence stays off screen: a grab, a scream, a cut to red.

**Themes.**

- **Inherited debt (*utang*):** what the family gained, someone always paid for. The bargain is the dark side of a family's fortune.
- **Keeping versus finishing:** Lola kept the cycle going out of fear. The cousins have to end it. Her last word to them is "finish it".
- **Grief at the lamay:** the whole night is a wake. The player is mourning Lola while learning who she really was.
- **Family turned against family:** in co-op, the people you love become the ones hunting you, until you save them.
